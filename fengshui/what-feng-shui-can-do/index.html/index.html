I Studied Feng Shui for 1 Month. Here's What Surprised Me.

So what can feng shui actually do? After a month of studying it as a complete beginner, my honest answer is: less than the sellers claim, more than the skeptics assume. The most useful piece I found is one school's key — the Eight Mansions "personal direction profile," a chart that tells you which compass directions supposedly suit you for sleeping and sitting. It is a traditional model, never verified by science, and my conclusion after a month is simple: it only deserves a try when trying costs nothing.

Everything below is my study notes, shared the way a classmate would — what the key is, how to use it on your bed and desk, how to test it on yourself, and where its limits are.

The key: your personal direction profile

Feng shui has several schools. Eight Mansions is just one of them, and its direction profile is one key, not the whole system. But it's the most personal part, because it answers a question most feng shui advice skips: directions for whom?

Here's how it works. Your birth year places you in one of two groups — East group or West group. Each group has four directions said to suit its people, and four to avoid. This is why generic advice online contradicts itself constantly: "point your bed north" is good advice for one group and bad advice for the other. Nobody asked which group you're in.

Why would a direction suit one person and not another? Honestly, I don't know — and after a month of digging, I never found a scientific explanation I could believe. Directions differing by person has not been verified by modern science. My verdict in one sentence: a traditional model; explore it if you're curious; its effects are unproven. That is exactly why it deserves one kind of use only — free experiments. Not your anxiety, and not your money.

You can find your group in about two minutes with the free calculator at rootbro.com/fengshui — you just need your birth date and gender.

The bed: which way your head points

You spend about a third of your life in bed, so the bed comes first. The rule is one sentence: when you sleep, your head should point toward one of your good directions, and stay out of your bad ones.

Notice the mirror image: what East group leans toward, West group avoids, and the other way around. Two people sharing a bed can have opposite charts — which, in this system, explains a few arguments about mattresses that were never really about the mattress.

The practical part is mercifully cheap. Bed can turn? Turn it. Can't turn it? Sleep the other way around, head at the foot end. Costs nothing either way. And if it can't be done at all, don't lose sleep over it — this is not insurance, and it is definitely not medicine.

Two bed rules don't depend on your group at all, and these rest on plain body sense rather than the model. First, put the headboard against a solid wall if you can, and be able to see the door from where you lie — resting with support at your back and the entrance in view lets your instincts stand down. Second, if you can, don't share the bed's wall with a bathroom: pipes bang in the night, and you're awake. That's not mysticism. That's sound insulation.

The desk: the Command Position

Second use of the key: the desk. Same rule — when you sit down to work, face one of your good directions. You spend nearly as many hours sitting as lying down, so in this system the desk is the second most important spot in your home.

The desk also has a universal rule, known in the West as the Command Position: solid wall behind you, entrance in view, never your back to the door. With your back to the door, part of your attention stays on guard duty all day — by evening, what wore you out may not be the work, but that low tension you can't quite name. Wall at your back, door in sight, and your shoulders drop on their own.

Try it properly: a one-week experiment

Trying this badly tells you nothing, so run it like an experiment, not a vibe.

  1. Baseline week. Don't move a thing. Live one full week as you are, and jot down your sleep and energy each day — which nights you slept deeply, which mornings your neck ached. Without a "before," every feeling afterward is imagination.
  2. Change one thing only: the bed. Check your head's direction against your chart. Already good? Change nothing. Pointing into a bad one? Turn the bed, or sleep the other way around. Leave the desk alone this week — one condition at a time.
  3. Observe one week. Keep the same notes. Then, only after that, run the desk through the same process if you want to continue.

And the fine print, up front: even if week two feels noticeably better, all you can honestly conclude is "this change may have helped me." Maybe you simply stopped staying up so late. That's fine. The point was never to prove the profile — it's to check whether your own days got more comfortable.

One rule outranks every direction on the chart: safety and convenience first. Never block a door with the bed to chase a direction. Never shove the desk into a airless corner. When the direction and the room fight, the room listens to you. You don't listen to the direction.

What feng shui cannot do

This is the part the people selling things will never tell you.

  • It cannot cure illness. If something is wrong with your body, see a doctor — non-negotiable. A quieter room, better blackout, fewer 3 a.m. jolts may help you sleep more soundly and rest a bit easier. That's an environment-and-habits effect, and it's where the claim ends. Anyone who says a furniture arrangement or a lucky object can cure a disease is running a scam.
  • It cannot make you rich. If an object could attract wealth, the seller would fill his own house with them instead of selling them to you. Money is earned, saved, and invested. None of it is arranged.
  • It cannot change your fate. At most, an object is a reminder of a decision you made. You did the work. If the object takes the credit, it's taking advantage of you.

I bought a golden feng shui toad years ago that was supposed to make me rich. Three years it sat by my door. It never made me a cent. The man who sold it, though — he did fine.

FAQ

How do I calculate my personal direction profile?

You need your birth date (year, month, and day — the exact date matters because the traditional cutoff is the start of spring, not January 1) and your gender, since the formula differs for men and women. The free calculator at rootbro.com/fengshui does the math in about two minutes and gives you your group plus your four good and four bad directions.

Does the direction my head points really matter when I sleep?

Within the Eight Mansions model, yes — it's the system's core sleeping rule. Outside the model, there is no scientific evidence that compass direction affects sleep. What is well supported is the environment layer: darkness, quiet, temperature, and a sleeping spot that feels secure. My honest take: treat the direction as a free experiment on yourself, never as a health claim.

I rent my place. Can I still use any of this?

Yes — that's actually where this key is most practical, because it works on movable things, not renovations. Turning a bed, sleeping the other way around, or rotating a desk costs nothing and leaves no trace when you move out. Skip anything that requires drilling, building, or buying.

What if I try it and feel no difference?

Then you've spent zero money and learned something real: this particular model didn't do anything noticeable for you. Keep your baseline notes as the evidence, keep whatever arrangement is more comfortable and convenient, and drop the rest without guilt. "Test, don't just believe" cuts both ways — a failed test is a result, not a failure of you.

How is this different from astrology?

Astrology reads your birth moment as a map of personality and timing. The personal direction profile is narrower and more mechanical: it doesn't describe who you are or predict what will happen — it only sorts compass directions into "suits you" and "avoid" for the two places you stay longest, your bed and your desk. Same traditional roots, much smaller claim.

Which direction is best, in general?

There isn't one — that's the whole point of the profile. "Best direction" is personal: the same compass point can be on one partner's good list and the other's avoid list. Any advice that gives everyone the same direction without asking for a birth date is skipping the step the entire system is built on.
